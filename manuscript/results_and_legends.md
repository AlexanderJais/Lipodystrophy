# Results

### Metreleptin corrects the metabolic derangements of lipodystrophy but not adipose tissue loss

We studied lipodystrophic *Ldlr*−/−;aP2-nSrebp1c-Tg mice (LD) treated with saline or metreleptin and non-lipodystrophic *Ldlr*−/− controls (WT), and analysed the hypothalamus by RNA sequencing at week 14 (Fig. 1a). At this time point, visceral (VAT) and subcutaneous (SAT) adipose depots were reduced by 80–90% in LD mice of both sexes (VAT, females 368 versus 45 mg, males 526 versus 61 mg; P < 0.001; Fig. 1c,d). Metreleptin did not restore adipose mass. Instead, it further reduced SAT in both sexes and VAT in females (Fig. 1c,d), and lowered body weight relative to WT (Fig. 1b).

In contrast, metreleptin corrected most of the metabolic consequences of lipodystrophy:

- **Liver weight:** increased 2.5-fold in saline-treated LD mice and reduced by metreleptin (Fig. 2a).
- **Plasma triglycerides:** increased 5-fold in saline-treated LD females and normalized by metreleptin. Saline-treated LD males had a similar median increase, but values were bimodal and the difference was not significant (Fig. 2b).
- **ALAT:** increased 5–7-fold in saline-treated LD mice and reduced by metreleptin in both sexes, although it remained above WT (Fig. 2c).
- **Triglyceride-rich lipoproteins:** VLDL and LDL triglycerides were increased 3–6-fold in saline-treated LD mice and returned to WT levels with metreleptin (Fig. 2d,e).
- **Free fatty acids:** increased in saline-treated LD females and normalized by metreleptin (Supplementary Fig. 1a).

HDL cholesterol changed in opposite directions in the two sexes. It was increased in saline-treated LD females and normalized by metreleptin, whereas it was decreased in saline-treated LD males (Fig. 2f). Plasma glucose did not differ between groups (Supplementary Fig. 1b). Thus, metreleptin was metabolically effective, whereas adipose tissue loss persisted.

### Lipodystrophy alters the hypothalamic transcriptome, predominantly in males

Compared with WT, saline-treated LD mice had 456 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in the hypothalamus in males (137 up, 319 down) but only 62 in females (28 up, 34 down) (Fig. 3a). Metreleptin-treated LD mice differed from WT at 297 genes in males and at 4 genes in females (Fig. 3a).

### Metreleptin normalizes a hypothalamic energy-deficit signature

Saline-treated LD males showed the arcuate signature of energy deficit, with increased expression of the orexigenic neuropeptides *Agrp* (log₂FC = 1.88, adjusted P = 1.9 × 10⁻⁶) and *Npy* (log₂FC = 1.07, adjusted P = 0.024) (Fig. 3b). Alongside these changes we found:

- induction of glucocorticoid-responsive genes (*Fkbp5*, *Zbtb16*, *Cdkn1a*, *Sult1a1*, *Plin4*, *Hif3a*; log₂FC 1.02–1.55);
- induction of the cold-inducible RNA-binding proteins *Rbm3* and *Cirbp*;
- an endothelial activation program (*Vwf*, *Icam1*, *Serpine1*, *Pecam1*, *Nos3*, *Klf4*, *Osmr*, *Tgm2*; log₂FC 0.44–1.49; endothelial cell-type enrichment q = 8 × 10⁻⁶).

None of these 18 genes differed between metreleptin-treated LD males and WT controls, and *Icam1* and *Tgm2* were significantly lower in metreleptin-treated than in saline-treated LD mice (Fig. 3b). Metreleptin-treated LD males had increased expression of the canonical leptin target *Socs3* (log₂FC = 0.99) and the leptin-responsive arcuate transcription factor *Nhlh2* (log₂FC = 0.62), confirming hypothalamic leptin signalling (Fig. 3b). In females, *Agrp* and *Npy* were not differentially expressed. However, *Rbm3* and *Vwf* were increased in saline-treated LD females, and metreleptin significantly corrected 11 genes that were altered in saline-treated LD females, including *Vgf*, *Podxl*, *Plxnd1*, *Nr2f2*, *Htra1* and *C4b*.

### In males, an oligodendrocyte and myelin deficit persists despite metreleptin

In parallel, saline-treated LD males showed coordinated downregulation of the oligodendrocyte lineage (log₂FC −0.30 to −1.03; Fig. 4a), spanning:

- lineage transcription factors (*Olig1*, *Olig2*, *Sox10*, *Myrf*);
- premyelinating oligodendrocyte markers (*Bcas1*, *Enpp6*, *Gpr17*);
- structural myelin components (*Mbp*, *Plp1*, *Mog*, *Mag*, *Mobp*, *Cnp*, *Cldn11*, *Opalin*);
- myelin lipid synthesis (*Ugt8a*, *Fa2h*, *Aspa*);
- glial gap junctions (*Gjc2*, *Gjb1*, *Gjc3*).

Homeostatic astrocyte genes (*Aqp4*, *Il33*, *Fabp7*, *Car2*, *Gpr37l1*) were also reduced, whereas markers of reactive astrocytes (*Gfap*, *Serpina3n*, *C3*) and activated microglia (*Aif1*, *Cd68*, *Trem2*) were unchanged.

Unlike the energy-deficit signature, the myelin deficit was not corrected by metreleptin:

- **Persistence vs WT:** in metreleptin-treated LD males, 23 of the 25 oligodendrocyte and myelin genes remained decreased relative to WT, with effect sizes nearly identical to those in saline-treated mice (median log₂FC −0.51 versus −0.55).
- **No leptin effect:** none of these genes differed between saline- and metreleptin-treated LD mice (Fig. 4a).
- **Genome-wide agreement:** across all 169 genes differentially expressed in both comparisons, the changes in metreleptin-treated LD males closely matched those in saline-treated LD males (Pearson r = 0.96), and every gene changed in the same direction (Fig. 4b). Genes that persisted under metreleptin were strongly enriched for oligodendrocyte markers (q = 1 × 10⁻⁵⁸).

In females, a subset of these genes (*Olig1*, *Bcas1*, *Mog*, *Mal*, *Cldn11*, *Qdpr*, *Gjc3*) was modestly reduced in saline-treated LD mice (log₂FC −0.23 to −0.36); none differed between metreleptin-treated LD females and WT, or between saline- and metreleptin-treated LD females (Fig. 4a).

### Lipodystrophy impairs terminal oligodendrocyte maturation while sparing precursors

To determine which stage of the oligodendrocyte lineage was affected, we examined stage-specific markers (Fig. 5a,b):

- **Precursors (OPC):** markers were largely unchanged in saline-treated LD males (*Pdgfra*, *Cspg4*, *Ptprz1*, *Vcan*, *Tnr*; only *Sox6* decreased).
- **Committed precursors (COP):** only *Gpr17* was decreased.
- **Newly formed oligodendrocytes (NFOL):** *Bcas1* and *Enpp6* were decreased.
- **Myelin-forming (MFOL) and mature (MOL) oligodendrocytes:** most markers were decreased (log₂FC −0.3 to −1.3).

The same stage profile was present in metreleptin-treated LD males, and no stage marker differed between saline- and metreleptin-treated LD mice (Fig. 5a,b). The lineage transcription factors *Olig1*, *Olig2*, *Sox10*, *Sox8*, *Myrf*, *Zeb2* and *Qki* were reduced in saline-treated LD males, and all except *Sox8* remained reduced under metreleptin (Fig. 5c). Cholesterol-biosynthesis genes (*Hmgcr*, *Sqle*, *Dhcr24*, *Srebf2*) were unchanged. Together, these data indicate impaired terminal maturation of oligodendrocytes rather than loss of the precursor pool.

### A sex-conserved glial signature and reduced fatty-acid synthesis

Sixteen genes were differentially expressed in saline-treated LD mice of both sexes, all in the same direction (Fig. 6a):

- 11 oligodendrocyte genes and 3 astrocyte genes, all decreased;
- *Vwf* and *Rbm3*, both increased.

Effect sizes in females were about half those in males (slope 0.53). In males, 11 of the 16 genes remained altered under metreleptin, whereas in females none differed between metreleptin-treated LD mice and WT (Fig. 6a).

Saline-treated LD males also showed reduced expression of fatty-acid desaturation and elongation genes (*Scd2*, *Elovl5*, *Elovl6*, *Hsd17b12*) and of *Me1*, which supplies NADPH for lipogenesis. All of these were normalized under metreleptin, except the myelin-associated elongase *Elovl7* (Fig. 6b). In females, only *Scd1* was reduced (Fig. 6b).

Thus, metreleptin corrects the systemic metabolic derangements and the hypothalamic energy-deficit state of lipodystrophic mice, but, like adipose tissue loss, the oligodendrocyte maturation deficit in males persists despite treatment.

# Key messages (slide version)

1. Metreleptin corrects the metabolic derangements of lipodystrophy (liver, triglycerides, ALAT) but not adipose tissue loss.
2. Lipodystrophy remodels the hypothalamic transcriptome, mainly in males (456 vs 62 DEGs).
3. The hypothalamic energy-deficit state is leptin-reversible. Metreleptin normalizes *Agrp*/*Npy* and the glucocorticoid-response, cold-inducible and endothelial programs. *Socs3* and *Nhlh2* confirm target engagement.
4. In males, oligodendrocyte maturation is impaired and this does not respond to metreleptin (r = 0.96). Precursors are spared, and myelin-forming and mature oligodendrocytes are affected.
5. The persistent glial deficit parallels persistent lipoatrophy, not the leptin-corrected metabolic state.
