# Metreleptin restores hypothalamic energy-balance signalling but does not rescue impaired oligodendrocyte maturation in lipodystrophic mice

## Abstract

Generalized lipodystrophy is characterized by the absence of functional adipose tissue and consequent leptin deficiency. Recombinant leptin (metreleptin) is an approved therapy that corrects most of the metabolic phenotype. Whether lipodystrophy also affects the central nervous system, and whether metreleptin corrects such changes, is unknown.

We performed RNA sequencing of the hypothalamus in male and female lipodystrophic *Ldlr*−/−;aP2-nSrebp1c-Tg mice treated with saline or metreleptin, and in non-lipodystrophic *Ldlr*−/− controls. Metreleptin corrected hepatomegaly, hypertriglyceridaemia and liver injury but, as expected, did not restore adipose tissue mass.

In the hypothalamus, lipodystrophic males showed an energy-deficit signature, with induction of *Agrp* and *Npy* together with glucocorticoid-responsive, cold-inducible and endothelial activation programs. Metreleptin normalized these programs and induced the leptin targets *Socs3* and *Nhlh2*.

In parallel, lipodystrophy caused a coordinated downregulation of the oligodendrocyte lineage: the transcriptional regulators *Sox10*, *Olig2* and *Myrf*, structural myelin genes, and myelin lipid-synthesis genes. Precursor markers were largely spared, whereas markers of myelin-forming and mature oligodendrocytes were broadly reduced, indicating impaired terminal maturation. In males, this deficit was not corrected by metreleptin: metreleptin did not change oligodendrocyte gene expression, and the lipodystrophy-induced changes persisted almost identically under treatment (r = 0.96). A milder, directionally concordant glial signature was present in females.

Thus, metreleptin restores hypothalamic energy-balance signalling but not oligodendrocyte maturation, which, like adipose tissue loss, persists despite treatment. These findings identify a leptin-independent central nervous system consequence of lipodystrophy with potential relevance for patients receiving leptin replacement therapy.
